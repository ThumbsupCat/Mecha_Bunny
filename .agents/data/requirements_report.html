0. Project Resume

The Mecha-Iepurele is an interactive robotic companion designed for indoor environments. It engages in natural conversations via a cloud AI API and physically tracks the user. The project features a dual-processor architecture, specialized mechanical hopping linkages, active steering controls, electrical isolation safety, and strict multi-user privacy protections.

Processing Core

  • MCXA153 Controller: Superloop processor. Handles ultrasonic tracking, PWM drives for 4x servos and 1x H-bridge, battery monitoring, and watchdog tasks.
  • ESP32-S3 Coprocessor: Handles Wi-Fi connection, HTTPS API calls, JSON parsing, direct I2S audio rendering (MAX98357A), and NVS wiping.
  • UART ACK Check: Software confirmation protocol ensures reliable command transmission between boards.

Locomotion & Shell

  • 90-Degree Kick Hopping: 1x central DC motor rotates a shared axle driving jointed leg linkages. When legs pivot to 90-degrees, they execute a gentle downward push to hop the chassis.
  • Active Steering: 1x SG90 steering servo pivots a rear tail-skid assembly left/right for turning.
  • Spacious Shell & Vents: PLA body shell (~180x120x120 mm) with ventilation grilles and magnetic latches. Sourced from pre-designed templates (no custom CAD modeling required).

Power & Privacy

  • Electrical Safety: 2S LiPo battery with separate digital/motor regulators and a 2S BMS protection board.
  • Onboard Charger: 2S balance charger connected to a shell-mounted USB-C port.
  • Privacy Shield: Wipes credentials from NVS (10-second button hold), zero-fills conversation RAM post-speech, and uses TLS encryption with isolated UUIDs.
  • Organic Gestures: Zero status LEDs; eyelid open/close and ear sweeps express states.

1. Input Interpretation and Selected Scope

The requirements and design choices detailed in this document are derived from the Agent 0 Scoping Brief, updated to incorporate the user's specific planning constraints. The target application is an interactive companion bunny featuring Text-to-Speech (TTS) voice output and local mechanical actuator control run by the NXP MCU, and high-level Wi-Fi connection, direct audio parsing, and JSON API resolution handled by the ESP32-S3 Nano.

Scope Parameters

Selected Feature Tier: Recommended Summer School Version

Intended User: Third-Year University CS Students

Operating Environment: Portable indoor environments requiring multi-user privacy protections and organic physical status feedback

Movement Style: Hopping mechanism mimicking a real bunny (driven by a single synchronized double-crank linkage) with active steering control and gentle, low-amplitude 90-degree rotary leg kicks

Refined Exclusions & Scoping Changes

  • Audio Parsing: Handled entirely on the ESP32-S3 Nano. Audio is non-transferable; no raw PCM streams are sent to the MCXA153, bypassing all MCU audio overhead.
  • Chassis & Jointed Leg Linkages: Driven by 1x central DC TT gear motor rotating a shared double-crank axle. Converts rotational motion to cyclic hopping. When the legs pivot through a 90-degree vertical position relative to the chassis, they execute a gentle downward push to lift the body slightly into a stable forward hop/scoot. Links are printed in tough PETG to handle impact fatigue. No sliding pistons or internal compression springs are used.
  • Bunny Shell & Battery Charging: Integrates a spacious body shell to accommodate the high density of internal components, featuring ventilation grilles, quick-release magnetic latches, and an onboard USB-C balance charger.
  • Steering System: To enable turns while preserving left/right leg phase lock (preventing tipping), the chassis incorporates a steerable rear tail-skid or caster assembly driven by a dedicated micro servo (SG90).
  • Status Indicators (No LEDs): Status LEDs are completely excluded. Wakefulness, sleep, active recording, low-power, and error states are conveyed through physical movements (eyelid open/close and ear sweeping/drooping) to maintain realism.
  • OLED & Web Input: Completely excluded. Connects directly to internet via Wi-Fi handshake.
  • Wi-Fi Portability & Privacy: Supports a provisioning mode for credential entry, a 10-second button hold sequence to wipe stored credentials, and immediate clearing of conversation RAM.
  • CAD Design / Timeline Disclaimer: The student has a total of 7 weeks to complete the project. Sourcing pre-designed, ready-to-slice 3D models (STL/STEP files) is highly recommended. If custom 3D model design is required, the student must budget a maximum of 1 week of the 7-week timeline to learn CAD tools (e.g. Blender) and model the parts.

3. Scenarios and User Stories

Scenarios

ID Category Description
SC-001 Startup / Setup The device is powered. The firmware initializes I/O pins, LPUART, and PWM timers on the MCXA153. The ESP32-S3 Nano reads Wi-Fi credentials from NVS and initiates a Wi-Fi connection handshake. Once connected, the ESP32 sends an "Online" status byte to the MCXA153, which opens the bunny's eyelids and sweeps the ears to confirm it is awake and online.
SC-002 Dynamic Wi-Fi Provisioning If the Wi-Fi connection fails within 15 seconds, or if the user holds the setup button for 5 seconds at boot, the ESP32-S3 Nano launches a setup Access Point. The bunny's eyelids blink slowly to suggest it is in Wi-Fi search mode. The user connects via phone, inputs credentials, and the bunny connects and opens its eyelids wide.
SC-003 Multi-User Device Wiping Tester A completes their session. Before handing the bunny to Tester B, Tester A holds down the physical setup button for 10 seconds. The ESP32-S3 Nano erases all stored Wi-Fi credentials, tokens, and caches from NVS, reboots, sweeps the ears to neutral, and closes the eyelids to sleep.
SC-004 Onboard Battery Charging The battery voltage drops below 6.4V, triggering sleep. The user plugs a standard 5V USB power adapter into the USB-C charging port mounted on the bunny's shell. The onboard 2S Balance Charger circuit charges and balances both LiPo cells, indicating charging progress via internal lighting visible through the ventilation grilles. Eyelids remain closed.
SC-005 Normal Operation The user triggers the conversation. The ESP32-S3 Nano queries the conversational AI API, receives the response text, mood, and servo coordinates, and parses the JSON body. The ESP32 extracts the servo targets and requests the synthesized speech audio stream from the cloud. Eyelids are open during interaction.
SC-006 Active Privacy Cleansing The voice response completes playback. The ESP32-S3 Nano immediately writes zeros over all local audio buffers and parses text strings in RAM. The next API request utilizes a newly generated, isolated session ID to prevent conversation profiling.
SC-007 Actuator Behavior (Ears & Eyelids) The ESP32-S3 Nano sends the parsed servo positions to the MCXA153 over LPUART. The MCXA153 updates its PWM registers on the FTM/TPM timers, driving the SG90 ear servos to sweep to the corresponding positions (e.g. drooping for sad, perking for happy) and keeping eyelids open.
SC-008 Sensor Processing During speech, the 50 ms motor loop executes on the MCXA153. The MCU triggers the ultrasonic sensor, measures the returned Echo pulse duration, and calculates user distance in centimeters.
SC-009 Proximity Hopping & Steering The motor control loop detects the user is at 80 cm, and slightly offset to the right. The MCXA153 drives the single central DC gear motor, rotating the shared double-crank axle. Both leg linkages rotate in phase. As they sweep to the 90-degree vertical position relative to the chassis, they execute a gentle, low-clearance downward push against the ground, executing a stable hop that lifts the body forward. Concurrently, it updates the steering servo PWM registers to pivot the rear tail-skid to the right, executing a smooth right turn to align with the user, slowing down as it reaches the 50 cm boundary.
SC-010 Safety & Reliability (Sleep Halt) The user presses the physical emergency stop button, or a timeout / low-battery threshold is reached. The MCXA153 immediately forces motor outputs to 0%, pulls STBY low, centers the steering servo, droops the ears, and closes the eyelids to indicate that the bunny is asleep.

6. Hardware and Mechanical Description

The Mecha-Iepurele hardware architecture features the NXP FRDM-MCXA153 development board as the central low-level processing unit, running all local motor control loops, servo ear/eyelid animations, and ADC readings. High-level network routing, API payload processing, and Text-to-Speech audio rendering are offloaded to the secondary ESP32-S3 Nano processing board.

Component Interconnection & Flow

  • Main Controller (MCXA153): Executes the main superloop, reading the ultrasonic sensor, checking battery level via ADC, and driving the actuators. Drives 2x ear servos, 1x eyelid servo, 1x steering servo, and 1x central DC motor H-bridge channel.
  • Coprocessor (ESP32-S3 Nano): Performs the Wi-Fi handshake, connects to the Internet, queries the cloud AI API, parses the JSON payload, directly outputs TTS voice via I2S, and monitors command ACKs from the MCXA153. Supports AP provisioning setup and NVS data-wiping.
  • MAX98357A I2S DAC & Speaker: Connected directly to the ESP32-S3 Nano's I2S pins. Amplifies and converts the digital voice stream locally.
  • Ultrasonic Distance Sensor: Connects via GPIOs to the MCXA153. A 3.3V compatible sensor (e.g. RCWL-1601) is used.
  • TB6612FNG H-bridge Driver: Drives the single high-torque DC TT gear motor based on PWM and direction outputs from the MCXA153.

Mechanical, Hopping & Steering Architecture

  • Hopping & Gentle Trajectory: A single central DC TT gear motor rotates a shared double-crank axle, driving jointed leg linkages (scissor or 4-bar swing type). Rather than telescoping internally, the legs act as rotary levers. As the motor turns the axle, the legs sweep downward. When they pass through the 90-degree vertical angle (straight down relative to the chassis), they deliver a gentle downward shove against the floor, launching the entire body off the ground to initiate a low-amplitude hop (5 to 15 mm vertical lift). This low-impact trajectory prevents chassis instability, avoids tipping, minimizes mechanical stress on linkages, and matches the torque capability of standard DC TT gear motors.
  • Steering Subsystem: To enable turns while preserving left/right leg phase lock (preventing tipping), the chassis incorporates a **steerable rear tail-skid or pivoting wheel assembly** driven by a dedicated SG90 micro servo. Pivoting this assembly left or right steers the chassis dynamically while the hopping linkages drive forward.
  • Joints & Bearings: Low-friction miniature ball bearings (MR85ZZ) or brass sleeve bushings are required at all pivoting pins.
  • Filament Selection (US-008):
    • PETG: Structural mounts, crank arms, steering brackets, and leg links to resist cyclic hopping landing impact fatigue.
    • TPU: Flexible feet traction pads and landing shock dampers.
    • PLA: Spacious decorative non-stress body shell (length ~180mm, width ~120mm, height ~120mm) featuring ventilation grilles and magnetic latches.
  • 3D CAD Assets & Timeline Lock: While pre-designed templates are highly recommended, the student may budget up to 1 week of the 7-week total project schedule to learn CAD tools (e.g. Blender) and perform custom 3D designs for linkages or cosmetic shells if necessary.
  • Power Supply & Charger: Powered by a shared 2S LiPo battery. Includes separate regulators for MCU and motor, and protective signal dividers. An onboard **2S balance charger module** (IP2326/TP5100) and **2S BMS protection board** are integrated, allowing safe USB-C balancing and charging of cells without removing the battery pack from the shell.

8. Firmware Architecture

Architecture Style: Asynchronous Dual-processor superloop execution with UART ACK checks

ESP32-S3 Nano Flow

Handles Wi-Fi provisioning and encrypted API transactions. Re-generates session UUIDs for user isolation. Runs a startup pin poll: if the setup button is held for 10 seconds, NVS is formatted. Zero-fills RAM buffers post-speech.

MCXA153 Firmware Modules

Runs a lightweight bare-metal superloop. Modules: main.c, uart_comm.c, motor_driver.c (drives single DC motor), servo_ears.c, eyelid_control.c, steering_control.c (steers tail-skid PWM), ultrasonic.c, and battery_adc.c.

MCXA153 Startup Sequence

  1. Disable global interrupts.
  2. Configure MCU system clocks to run at maximum speed (48 MHz).
  3. Initialize GPIO ports, enabling internal pull-ups for inputs and setting output pins to default logic levels (motor off, driver in standby).
  4. Configure LPUART peripheral (115200, 8-N-1) and enable LPUART RX interrupts.
  5. Configure FTM/TPM timers: TPM0 for DC motor PWM (10 kHz carrier) and TPM1 for ear, eyelid, and steering servos PWM (50 Hz).
  6. Configure ADC peripheral (12-bit resolution) and calibrate the channel connected to the battery voltage divider.
  7. Initialize SysTick timer to generate interrupts every 1 ms.
  8. Enable global interrupts.
  9. Confirm handshake from ESP32-S3 Nano, then transition to IDLE. Eyelids open, ears sweep to neutral, steering centers.

MCXA153 Main Superloop Flow

  • Task 1: Command Parser & ACK: If a complete LPUART packet is received from the ESP32:
    • Validate checksum / CRC.
    • If valid, transmit an ACK byte (e.g. 0x06) back to the ESP32-S3 Nano.
    • Update ear servo PWM registers, eyelid positions, and steering angles based on parameters.
  • Task 2: Motor Control, Steering & Battery Poll (Every 50 ms):
    • Trigger the ultrasonic sensor, measure Echo, and compute distance.
    • If Echo does not update for 250 ms, enter the safe state (disable PWM, center steering, close eyelids, droop ears).
    • Calculate error: error = measured_distance - target_distance_center.
    • Adjust motor PWM output and steering angle output using proportional gain coefficients.
    • If distance sensor is out of range (< 10 cm or > 150 cm), force motor to stop, center steering, and close eyelids.
    • Poll battery ADC. If voltage reads < 6.4 V, disable motor, droop ears, center steering, and close eyelids to sleep.
  • Task 3: Watchdog Feed: Reset the coprocessor watchdog timer.

Error Handling & Safe-State Strategy (Organic Sleep)

If the LPUART link between the MCU and the ESP32 drops for more than 3 seconds, or if the battery ADC reads below 6.4V, or if the ultrasonic sensor software timeout triggers (no updates for 250 ms), the MCXA153 enters the SAFE_SHUTDOWN state. The motor is immediately stopped, steering centers, the driver STBY line is pulled low, ears are drooped, and eyelids are closed completely to indicate the bunny is asleep.

  • The power subsystem incorporates a panel-mounted USB-C port, an onboard **2S balance charger module** (FR-023, TP5100/IP2326), and a **2S BMS board** (NFR-010, R-009) to charge and balance the battery cells safely without removal from the shell.
  • A mechanical CAD dependency disclaimer (NFR-011, R-011, chk-d) has been formalised: Sourcing pre-designed mechanical templates (STL/STEP) is highly recommended. If custom 3D model design is required, the student has a strict maximum budget of 1 week of the 7-week schedule to learn CAD tools (e.g. Blender) and design the parts.
  • An active steering subsystem (FR-025, TC-020, R-012, chk-e) has been added: An SG90 steering servo pivots a rear tail-skid assembly left/right by ± 30 degrees, allowing the hopping bunny chassis to take motion turns smoothly while maintaining main leg axle synchronization.
  • Rotary 90-degree kick locomotion with gentle trajectory (FR-026, TC-021, R-013, chk-f) has been defined: Rather than a telescopic piston guide, the legs act as jointed levers. As the motor rotates the shared axle, the legs sweep downward. When they pass through the 90-degree vertical position relative to the chassis, they execute a gentle downward push against the floor, launching the entire body slightly off the ground (5 to 15 mm vertical lift) to initiate a low-amplitude, stable forward scoot/hop. TPU pads on the feet absorb the landing impact, keeping fabrication simple and protecting components.
